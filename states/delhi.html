╰┈➤History of Delhi

Delhi, the capital of India, holds a rich and diverse history that spans millennia. Its story begins with the rise of the Indus Valley Civilization around 2500 BCE, evidenced by archaeological findings in the region. Over centuries, Delhi evolved as a strategic location, witnessing the rise and fall of numerous empires and dynasties.

One of the earliest significant periods in Delhi's history was during the Mauryan Empire (322–185 BCE), when the city was known as Indraprastha, according to Hindu mythology. Later, Delhi gained prominence during the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526), established by various Turkic and Afghan dynasties. The most notable of these was the Mughal Empire (1526–1857), which left an indelible mark on the city's architecture, culture, and society. Under Mughal rule, Delhi flourished as a center of art, commerce, and learning.

However, Delhi's history also saw periods of turmoil and invasions, notably by Central Asian conquerors such as Timur in the 14th century. The city faced successive waves of conquests, including those by Persian ruler Nadir Shah and Afghan ruler Ahmad Shah Durrani in the 18th century, leading to its plunder and destruction.

In the 19th century, Delhi came under British colonial rule after the East India Company's victory in the Indian Rebellion of 1857. The British made Delhi the capital of British India in 1911, replacing Kolkata. This period marked significant urban development and administrative changes in Delhi.

After India gained independence from British rule in 1947, Delhi became the capital of the newly formed Republic of India. The city underwent rapid modernization and expansion, with the establishment of government institutions, diplomatic missions, and infrastructure projects. In 1991, Delhi was officially designated as the National Capital Territory of India (NCT), with a special status granted for its administration.

Today, Delhi stands as a vibrant metropolis, blending its ancient heritage with modernity. It remains a cultural hub, boasting iconic landmarks such as the Red Fort, Qutub Minar, and Humayun's Tomb, alongside bustling markets, educational institutions, and political centers. As one of the world's most populous cities, Delhi continues to evolve, reflecting its enduring legacy as a symbol of India's past, present, and future.
